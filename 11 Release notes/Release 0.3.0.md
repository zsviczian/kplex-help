# Canvas views and reusable workflows

Choose a perspective that suits the work in front of you. Explore a note’s context with Compass, develop a hierarchy with Mind Map, or organize connected notes through your own Facets workflow.

## Keep your familiar map, choose another perspective

**Compass** is the name of the default directional view. Each canvas can choose its own view through **Views and lenses**, so one topic can have a relationship map alongside a hierarchy or classified neighborhood. Shared navigation and editor defaults live under **Canvas behavior**; view-specific arrangement and presets live in its **Views** group.

See [[01 Core concepts/Canvas views|the view comparison]] to choose a starting point.

## Make useful classifications visible

**Facets** groups the current note's direct neighbors using a property, tags, note types or category rules. Define three to seven categories, then choose a circular, concentric, horizontal, vertical or quadrant layout. Resize category areas, position diagnostic areas and add workflow guides or captions where they help explain the sequence.

Create a workflow for your own notes, then keep its explanation with the preset. The Help vault's four working demonstrations show project stages, topics, learning routes and a two-property workbench. [[04 Filter and focus/Facets|Start with a question of your own]].

## Reuse a workflow without copying notes

Copy or download a Facets preset or Graph Lens as JSON. Review an import before adding or replacing an entry. Choose companion lenses explicitly; referenced palette colors travel with the definition. Preset help travels with the definition; the notes it describes stay in their vault.

See [[04 Filter and focus/Share presets and lenses|sharing presets and lenses]].

## Develop ideas without losing the outline

**Mind Map** keeps a stable root while you move keyboard selection and unfold branches. Click a note or press Enter to center it and start its map; use a chevron or Alt/Option+Enter to fold a branch without centering it. Choose a balanced, left or right child tree, expand one generation at a time and keep parent context above the root. Parent areas grow upward to accommodate their branches; pan and zoom to reach the rest of a longer map.

Open an eligible Markdown note for editing where it sits. Compass also supports editors on neighboring notes without moving the center. There is one node editor at a time: opening another or switching views saves and closes the existing editor; if saving fails, it stays available for recovery.

See [[02 Navigate and explore/Mind Map|Mind Map]] and [[02 Navigate and explore/Editor node and web pages|reading and writing inside the canvas]].

## Reveal structure inside a long note

Expand Markdown headings in Compass or at Mind Map's root. Section folds and graph branches are independent, so you can inspect the note's internal structure without changing the recorded relationships. Facets keeps its focus on classification and does not show section trees.

See [[04 Filter and focus/Expand a note into sections|expanding a note into sections]].
