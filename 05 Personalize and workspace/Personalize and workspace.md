Change the **appearance and reading layout** of a Plex without changing the meaning of its underlying relationships. Guides distinguish released behavior from next-release additions.

## Make the map readable

- [[05 Personalize and workspace/Layout density and typography]] — tune compactness, wrapping and node width; next-release One-sided layout offers a narrower arrangement.
- [[05 Personalize and workspace/Node styles from help tags]] — make concepts, tasks, paths and cautions visually distinct.
- [[05 Personalize and workspace/Images in nodes]] — use local thumbnails or intentionally permit remote node images.
- [[05 Personalize and workspace/Visibility and connector styling]] — choose what appears and use next-release modifier previews to inspect cross-links without keeping them all visible.

**Next release:** [[05 Personalize and workspace/Colors and themes]] explains native Obsidian colors, independent named K-Plex themes, Light/Dark pairs, directional tint and separate base/semantic color resets. Existing appearances are preserved during migration.

## Keep context beside the map

[[05 Personalize and workspace/Sidecar]] provides a normal document pane and explains next-release folding of the complete Plex tab group. [[05 Personalize and workspace/Note-tab synchronization]] distinguishes document following from a fixed reference tab and explains why multiple Plex views are not yet independent sync pairs.

**Try this:** Inspect the help's tag-based styles, then change density or label wrapping in Configure Plex layout. For an actual project, use [[07 Use cases/Projects and decisions]]; for visual notes, try [[07 Use cases/Develop visual ideas]].

[[05 Personalize and workspace/Fullscreen and Zen mode]] covers maximizing the desktop workspace or hiding toolbars without discarding camera context. [[05 Personalize and workspace/Actions and shortcuts]] separates local bindings from actions published to Obsidian's Command Palette.
