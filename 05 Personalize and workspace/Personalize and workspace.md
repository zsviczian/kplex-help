# Personalize and workspace

Want the map and your reading space to fit the way you work? Tune readability, choose useful visual cues and keep the right document beside the graph.

## Make the map readable

- [[Layout density and typography]] — tune compactness, wrapping and node width; One-sided layout offers a narrower arrangement.
- [[Node styles from help tags]] — make concepts, tasks, paths and cautions visually distinct.
- [[Images in nodes]] — use local thumbnails or intentionally permit remote node images.
- [[Visibility and connector styling]] — choose what appears and use modifier previews to inspect cross-links without keeping them all visible.

[[Colors and themes]] explains native Obsidian colors, independent named K-Plex themes, Light/Dark pairs, directional tint and separate base/semantic color resets. Existing appearances are preserved during migration.

## Keep context beside the map

[[Sidecar]] provides a normal document pane and explains folding of the complete canvas tab group. [[Note-tab synchronization]] distinguishes document following from a fixed reference tab and explains how each canvas keeps its own document pairing.

**Try this:** Inspect the help's tag-based styles, then change density or label wrapping in Layout controls. For an actual project, use [[Projects and decisions]]; for visual notes, try [[Develop visual ideas]].

## Workflows and display modes

[[Fullscreen and Zen mode]] covers maximizing the desktop workspace or hiding toolbars without discarding camera context. [[Actions and shortcuts]] separates local bindings from actions published to Obsidian's Command Palette.

Follow [[07 Customize the look and workspace]], [[13 Present and concentrate]] or [[11 Work without the mouse]] for a guided route.

## Choose a perspective

[[Canvas views]] helps you choose Compass, Mind Map or categories that fit the task.
