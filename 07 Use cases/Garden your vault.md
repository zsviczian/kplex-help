**Outcome:** A small part of your vault becomes easier to understand and reuse, with less unfinished thinking and fewer unexplained connections.

**Status:** Available in 0.1.3. Following the current note in File properties and graph-based folder/tag assignment are next-release improvements.

## Try it: tend one neglected topic

Choose one project or subject rather than trying to clean the entire vault. Add a simple property such as `maturity: seedling` to notes that need development. A separate property such as `structure: needs-links` can distinguish an unfinished idea from one that merely needs better connections. These are your own example values, not built-in K-Plex scores.

Create a **Notes → Style matching** Graph Lens for one of these values. Explore the topic and improve one highlighted note at a time: explain its main idea, connect its source, add a useful parent, or record a question that deserves its own note. Inspect an unexpected connection with **Connection details…** before removing anything.

Update the property's value when the note becomes useful. An Obsidian Base can provide a vault-wide table for finding candidates and editing those same properties; K-Plex supplies the surrounding context while you work.

**Done when:** You have improved a handful of notes and can find them through a meaningful topic or project, rather than only by remembering their filenames.

## Keep the review honest

Graph Lenses evaluate the current Plex neighborhood. They do not scan your whole vault for disconnected notes or automatically assess note quality. Use Bases or Obsidian search to find candidates outside the current neighborhood, then explore them in K-Plex.

**Next release:** An unpinned, ungrouped File properties pane follows the central file. Supported tag and folder drops can also change tags or move files; these are real note/file edits, not just visual grouping. See [[03 Build and edit/Drag drop and reclassify]] and [[05 Personalize and workspace/Note-tab synchronization]].
