The **Challenger** region is useful beyond simple “cons.” It can hold any idea that improves thinking by pushing against the center:

- [counter-arguments](https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/counterargument);
- [disconfirming evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_bias_mitigation);
- [alternative designs](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/17/15/7142);
- [risks and failure modes](http://homepages.se.edu/cvonbergen/files/2013/01/Performing-a-Project-Premortem.pdf);
- [trade-offs that cannot be optimized away](https://nissenbaum.tech.cornell.edu/papers/Rethinking%20Optimization:%20A%20Systems-Based%20Approach%20to%20Social%20Externalities.pdf).

Friends can hold supporting or analogous material while Challengers remain visibly separate. This creates a lightweight argument map without introducing a special diagram format.

For richer analysis, use ontology names such as `Supports` → Friend and `Opposes` → Challenger, then style those connector definitions or build Graph Lenses around them.
