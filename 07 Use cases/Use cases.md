# What You Can Do with K-Plex

Put your notes to work, rather than learn controls one by one. Choose a result you care about and try a small workflow that gets you there.

![[Image - Use cases.png]]


Start with something you want to accomplish, not a feature you want to try. These everyday workflows turn existing notes and their relationships into useful results. Each guide includes a concrete example, a short workflow and a clear finishing point.

## Start here: never start a project with a blank page

**[[Never start a project with a blank page|Bring your existing knowledge into a new project]].** Pin a new project, discover relevant notes by browsing, through a second canvas, Obsidian Bases or the file explorer, or let Wander surface unexpected inspiration. Connect notes as you encounter them—without needing to design the perfect Map of Content in advance. The guide connects the workflow to the relevant feature references.

## More practical outcomes

1. **[[Build a Map of Content|Turn scattered notes into a useful topic map]].** Gather a subject around a Map of Content as you explore, so you have a reliable place to begin next time.
2. **[[Projects and decisions|Know where a project stands and what to do next]].** Bring goals, deliverables, decisions and risks together; keep the results of meaningful activities as part of the project's history.
3. **[[Research and learning|Turn research into an article]].** Develop a structure for your argument while keeping its claims connected to sources, examples and counter-evidence.
4. **[[Argument maps with Challengers|Challenge an assumption before acting]].** Examine a proposal from several directions and record why you accepted, changed or rejected it.
5. **[[Meeting notes and daily notes|Reconstruct what happened and why]].** Move between projects, meetings and daily notes to recover the context of a decision or a week of work.
6. **[[Sequential workflows|Follow a procedure without losing its context]].** Navigate an intentional sequence while keeping each step's instructions, examples and exceptions nearby.
7. **[[Garden your vault|Garden your vault — one note at a time]].** Pin tags for quick classification, refine maturity or status in the following File properties pane, and use Graph Lenses to see improvements as you work.
8. **[[Rediscover and remember your notes|Make forgotten ideas useful again]].** Use Wander for rediscovery or manual recall, then connect an old insight to something you are working on now.
9. **[[Develop visual ideas|Turn a rough sketch into an idea you can explain]].** Develop visual notes with Excalidraw, connect their context and objections, and walk someone through the resulting reasoning.
10. **[[Bring a connected outline into your writing|Start writing from the map you have already built]].** Export a linked outline, share a PNG, or give a local assistant the context of one chosen canvas.

## Make the pattern your own

Choose one workflow and a small set of notes. Add relationships because they answer a useful question, not to fill every direction. Custom field names in these guides are examples to configure, not a required ontology.

For an advanced documentation example, open [[Build a help system like this vault]]. For guided feature lessons, use [[Choose a topic]], especially [[04 Research and challenge ideas]] and [[05 Organize projects and sequences]].
